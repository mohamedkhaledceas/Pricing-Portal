Exchange rates

How many EGP one unit is worth. Salaries, expenses and third-party costs can each be entered in their own currency, and any project can be quoted to the client in another — but the cost and the margin are always worked out in EGP, so a weak month for the pound never flatters a quote. Use the rate your bank actually gives you, not the mid-market rate.

The minimum margin is the line the team view will not let anyone cross quietly: price above target shows green, between target and minimum shows amber, below the minimum shows red. Neither the cost nor the margin figure is ever displayed in that view.

How people's hourly cost is calculated

The recovery rate is the honest one: if a designer costs 20,000/month and only 120 of her 176 hours are sellable, every sellable hour has to carry 20,000 ÷ 120 = 167, not 20,000 ÷ 176 = 114. Using the raw rate quietly loses you the difference on every quote.

How the numbers work

Cost per hour = (salary + extra employer cost) ÷ billable hours per month.
Overhead per hour = all fixed expenses ÷ all billable hours the team can sell in a month. Every project hour carries this, so rent and software are paid for by the work.
True project cost = labour + overhead + direct costs + contingency.
Suggested price = true cost ÷ (1 − target margin). Note this is a margin, not a mark-up: to keep 35% you divide by 0.65, you do not add 35%.
Margin = (price − true cost) ÷ price. Mark-up = (price − true cost) ÷ true cost.
Capacity = each project's hours spread evenly across the months it runs, from its start date, against each person's billable hours. Over 100% in a month means that month is oversold.
Break-even = your monthly burn. Any month you bill less than that, the company is losing money regardless of how good individual project margins look.
